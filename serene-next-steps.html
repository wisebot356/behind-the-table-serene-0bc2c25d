The idea

The practice should support the person doing the work.

Massage therapists learn to care for clients. The schedule, paperwork, money, and everyday decisions around that care can be another job entirely.

Behind the Table gives them space to notice what’s working, what’s taking too much, and one small change worth considering. A smaller, well-balanced practice is just as valid as a growing one.

Provider Solutions offers billing and administrative services. We’ll say that plainly, without treating those services—or insurance—as the answer for everyone.

What a therapist will experience

A short check-in. A useful next move. No pressure.

They answer eight questions on their phone, see a suggested next step, and can use a simple worksheet to think it through. They don’t have to give us an email address or agree to a sales conversation to see their result.

“Did this fit your situation—and did it help you see something useful?”

That’s what we want to learn from the first five people. We’re not asking them to buy anything.

What happens next

Finish, try, listen, improve.

  1. Finish the experience

    The questions and draft results are written. Next we’ll make the phone version easy to use and finish the practical worksheets it promises.

  2. Check it ourselves first

    Make sure the questions, suggested next steps, and links work properly. Agree on who can see the answers, how long we keep them, and how someone can ask us to remove them.

  3. Invite five people we trust

    Once the experience and invitation are approved, ask five therapists to join a short feedback conversation—about 20 minutes each. Let them try it without explaining their answers for them.

  4. Learn before sharing more widely

    Ask what felt useful, confusing, or too personal. Improve what needs work before considering a wider invitation or gathering.

We’re not starting from scratch.

We already have the question set, five possible next-step results, invitation and social-message drafts, a visual direction, and a guide for the feedback conversations.

What still needs finishing: the working phone experience, the worksheets, and the practical arrangements for looking after answers and inviting the first five people.

A big launch, ads, videos, and a gathering date can wait. The first job is to make this useful.
